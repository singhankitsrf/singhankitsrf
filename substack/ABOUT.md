# About — Evidence-First AI Systems

**Evidence-First AI Systems** is a practitioner-focused publication about building AI systems that can survive the transition from a research idea or prototype to an inspectable, testable and responsibly operated engineering system.

I write about four connected areas: **reliable medical AI, agentic AI and RAG platforms, AI platform engineering, and the path from PhD research to production-oriented implementation**.

The central idea is simple: an AI claim should be proportional to its evidence.

A model architecture is not a measured result. Source code is not proof of production reliability. A static cloud diagram is not evidence of deployed latency or cost. A high aggregate accuracy does not by itself establish calibration, robustness or suitability for a high-stakes workflow.

That distinction shapes the way I design and communicate AI systems.

## What you will find here

### Reliable Medical AI
Articles on evaluation, calibration, uncertainty-aware routing, leakage control, explainability, human review, multimodal systems and responsible research-to-deployment boundaries.

### Agentic AI & RAG Platforms
Systems-level discussions of LangGraph, LangChain, MCP, retrieval, tool governance, human approval, evaluation, observability and security for agentic applications.

### AI Platform Engineering
Practical architecture around MLOps, model lifecycle management, AWS SageMaker, Azure data platforms, PySpark, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform, CI/CD and release provenance.

### PhD → Production
Lessons on turning research questions into reproducible engineering artifacts, communicating evidence clearly, and designing projects that are scientifically defensible and technically useful.

## How I work

My public engineering portfolio is organized around five flagship systems spanning agentic AI, medical computer vision, AWS AI platforms, NLP ReleaseOps and Azure/PySpark data engineering.

I deliberately separate architecture intent, implemented source code, reproducible offline evaluation, cloud-executed evidence, deployed-system evidence, production claims and external validation.

That makes the work easier to inspect and keeps technical communication honest.

## Who this publication is for

Evidence-First AI Systems is written for AI/ML engineers, research scientists, architects, technical leaders, PhD researchers and practitioners who care about what happens after a promising model or prototype exists.

If you are interested in AI systems that are measurable, reproducible, governed and designed with clear evidence boundaries, this publication is for you.

## Publishing cadence

I publish one substantial technical article approximately every two weeks, with shorter engineering and research notes between long-form posts.
